AI SYSTEMS

Where AI belongs in a business workflow

AI is most useful when it owns a narrow, valuable task inside a workflow — not when every step is handed to a model.

X10 Think5 min read

Look for cognitive friction

Good AI candidates often involve summarising, classifying, extracting, drafting, searching across knowledge or proposing options. The task should be expensive enough to matter but bounded enough to verify.

If a deterministic rule can solve the problem reliably, use the rule. AI should earn its place.

Keep inputs and outputs structured

An AI feature becomes easier to operate when the system knows what goes in and what shape should come out. Structured prompts, schemas, validation and clear UI states reduce ambiguity.

The surrounding product should handle permissions, data, workflow state and business rules. The model should not silently become the database or the policy engine.

Design the review point

Higher-impact decisions benefit from a human confirmation step. The interface should make it obvious what the AI suggested, what source context was used and what the user can change.

This creates accountability and makes failures recoverable instead of invisible.

Plan for failure

A production workflow needs useful behaviour when a model is slow, unavailable or uncertain. Fallback paths, retry limits and safe defaults are product features, not edge cases.

Working principle

Choose the smallest clear system that can prove the workflow, then expand from evidence.
