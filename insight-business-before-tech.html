PRODUCT STRATEGY

Business before technology: a better starting point for software

The fastest way to create unnecessary software is to start with a list of features before the business problem is understood.

X10 Think5 min read

Start with the change, not the stack

A useful software brief can often begin with one sentence: what should become easier, faster, safer or more visible after this system exists? That question creates a decision boundary. It separates the outcome from the implementation.

A stack, framework or AI model may become important later, but choosing it first can quietly force the problem to fit the technology.

Map the workflow that exists today

Before designing screens, map what people actually do now: where information starts, who touches it, where decisions happen, what is duplicated and what causes delay. This turns vague frustration into observable steps.

The best early scope is usually not “digitise everything.” It is the smallest connected workflow where a product can create a complete improvement.

Define evidence before build

Success criteria do not need to be complex. Time saved, fewer hand-offs, fewer errors, faster response, clearer status or higher completion can all be useful signals.

When evidence is defined early, product trade-offs become easier. Features that do not contribute to the outcome can be postponed instead of becoming permanent complexity.

Then choose the technology

Once the workflow, users, constraints and evidence are clear, architecture becomes a response to the problem rather than a preference. That is where technology becomes leverage instead of decoration.

Working principle

Choose the smallest clear system that can prove the workflow, then expand from evidence.
